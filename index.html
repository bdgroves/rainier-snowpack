Rain or snow? The freezing level

A storm that brings rain to 6,000 ft and one that brings snow to 2,000 ft can carry the same water. The freezing level is estimated by fitting the stations' latest temperatures against their elevations.

Where the water goes

Four USGS gauges on rivers that drain the mountain, last 7 days, hourly. Winter floods here come from atmospheric rivers — warm, wet storms that rain on the snowpack. Watch the freezing level and these lines together.

The combined chart puts each river as a % of its approximate minor-flood flow, so a creek and a big river share one axis (the flood line appears once any river passes 40%). Those flows are rough, for colour only — the NWS Seattle river forecasts are the authority.
Data freshness
How it works. Every hour a GitHub Action pulls daily and hourly readings from the NRCS AWDB API (with NRCS's 1991–2020 medians), the station records back to 1979, NOAA's ONI and ENSO outlook, NASA MODIS (Terra, Aqua as backup) via Earthdata, Sentinel-2 via Planetary Computer, and USGS streamflow from the Water Data API. The Rainier average is Σ SWE ÷ Σ median over the mountain's stations reporting both, so a missing station can't move it. % of median waits until the median itself passes 1″ — early-season percentages of a tiny number mean nothing.
